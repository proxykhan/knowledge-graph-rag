The problem

Some questions can't be answered from any single page. "Who supplies the competitors of Micron's customers?" needs three facts that sit in three different annual reports. Ordinary AI search reads the few passages that best match the question, so it never sees the whole chain, and it scored 0% on questions like this.

Ordinary AI search

Like a librarian who finds you the best-matching page. Great for single facts, lost when the answer is spread across several reports.

This system

Like a detective with a wall of photos and red strings. It knows how everyone is connected, follows the strings, and shows you the sentence behind each one.

The example question, as the system sees it

Micron supplies NVIDIA (from NVIDIA's report) → Intel and AMD compete with NVIDIA (NVIDIA's report) → TSMC, ASML and others supply Intel and AMD (Intel's and AMD's reports).

What happens, step by step

1

Read the reports

Each annual report is split into short passages. An AI reads every passage and lists the relationships it states, keeping the exact sentence as proof. Anything it can't quote is thrown away.

2

Draw the map

"TSMC", "Taiwan Semiconductor" and "Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited" are merged into one dot. The result is a map of 424 people, companies and products, and 454 links.

3

Pick where to look

For each question, a quick AI step decides: follow the map (connection questions), search the report text (single facts), or both when unsure.

4

Answer, then check

The answer must cite a source for every statement. Citations that weren't really found, or numbers not in the source, are rejected and the answer is rewritten. If the reports don't say it, it refuses.

Where it still goes wrong

  • Questions mixing two kinds of link ("which of NVIDIA's suppliers does Micron call competitors?") need a lookup it doesn't have yet; ordinary search got that one right.
  • When search misses the right passage, it refuses rather than guess.
  • Long lists can come back incomplete.
Honest caveat: the original 50 questions were written with the map in view, and the latest fixes were designed after studying their failures, so 86% is optimistic. The 15 new questions are the fairer test, but they were still written by someone who had seen the map. Questions from a person who hasn't would be the final check.
